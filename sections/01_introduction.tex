\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Students and knowledge-content editors often know what a short clip should explain but still have to locate useful moments, assemble them, and inspect the export. ClipAgent accepts one video and one brief and returns a playable MP4, optional subtitles, and a source-linked edit decision list (EDL). A follow-up can revise the duration, aspect ratio, or requested content. Each version retains its plan and earlier downloads. Related systems informed the dialogue flow, evidence retrieval, and review stages \citep{cliptalk,videoagent,videouse,openmontage}.

The proposal called for evidence retrieval, stateful planning, and verified execution. The prototype delivers those functions for one source through its English VideoClip interface. The main comparison uses four received third-party English explainers from an original plan for 32 self-recorded concept explanations and software demonstrations. We separately tested the interactive workflow on a synthetic spreadsheet exercise. We deferred multi-source editing, hybrid vector retrieval, word-level alignment, distributed execution, and enterprise authorization.
